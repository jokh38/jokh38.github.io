Scanning proton therapy offers advantages over conventional photon therapy due to the Bragg-peak effect and superior dosimetric precision, but compared with passive methods it tends to exhibit a wider penumbra in the low-energy range. This study evaluated the effectiveness of a patient-specific aperture collimator (AC) and multi-leaf collimator (MLC) for the line scanning proton treatment system at the Samsung Proton Therapy Center in Korea. The penumbra reduction efficiency of the collimation devices and the associated neutron dose were assessed through Monte Carlo simulations, and clinical effectiveness was evaluated dosimetrically and through quality assurance. The AC displayed the highest efficiency in penumbra reduction, reducing penumbra width to 43.75% of the no-collimator case at a depth of 10 cm with a 150 MeV proton beam, while the MLC also presented a comparable effect, reducing penumbra width to 56.25%.
