Purpose: The purpose of this study was to investigate the feasibility of two-dimensional (2D) dose distribution deconvolution using convolutional neural networks (CNNs) instead of an analytical approach for an in-house scintillation detector that has a detector-interface artifact in the penumbra region. Methods: 2D dose distribution datasets were collected from a Novalis Tx medical linear accelerator, including various radiation field sizes and 13 clinical IMRT plans. PenumbraNet, a shallow linear CNN model, was developed and trained to convert measured dose distributions to treatment planning system calculations. Performance was tested using open field and clinical IMRT cases, evaluated with gamma index criteria. Results: The mean gamma passing rates were 84.77% and 95.81% with 3%/3 mm gamma criteria for the measured and deconvolved distributions, respectively, using the linear PenumbraNet; the nonlinear variant achieved a 96.62% passing rate, an improvement of 11.85% over the measured distribution at the 3%/3 mm criteria. Conclusions: The feasibility of the PenumbraNets for 2D dose distribution deconvolution was demonstrated.
