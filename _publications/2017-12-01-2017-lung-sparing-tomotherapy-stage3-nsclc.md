Radiation pneumonitis (RP) has been a challenging obstacle in treating stage III lung cancer patients. Beam angle optimization (BAO) technique for Tomotherapy was developed to reduce the normal lung dose for stage III non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Comparative analyses on plan quality by 3 different Intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) methods with BAO were done, using ten consecutive stage IIIB NSCLC patients receiving linac-based static IMRT (L-IMRT) with a total of 66 Gy in 33 fractions; two additional Tomotherapy-based IMRT plans (helical beam and static beam) were generated for each patient, with beam angles optimized using complete and directional block functions in Tomotherapy. The best PTV coverage was achievable by helical tomotherapy IMRT (TH-IMRT), with a conformity index of 0.82 and homogeneity index of 1.06, followed by static beam tomotherapy IMRT (TD-IMRT) and linac-based static IMRT. As PTV length increased, NTCP and mean lung dose proportionally and significantly increased in all three plans, indicating that PTV dimensions significantly influence radiation pneumonitis risk regardless of technique selection.
