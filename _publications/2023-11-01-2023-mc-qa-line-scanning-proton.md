Background: Quality assurance (QA) is a prerequisite for safe and accurate pencil-beam proton therapy. Conventional measurement-based patient-specific QA (pQA) can only verify limited aspects of patient treatment and is labor-intensive. Purpose: Line scanning, which involves continuous and rapid delivery of pencil beams, is a state-of-the-art proton therapy technique whose machine performance depends on the complexity of beam modulations and contributes to patient treatment accuracy. A Monte Carlo (MC) simulation-based QA method reflecting the uncertainty related to the machine during scanning beam delivery was developed and verified for clinical applications to pQA. Methods: A tool for particle simulation (TOPAS) was used for nozzle modeling and commissioned against measurements. Machine log files recorded every 60 microseconds by monitors downstream of the nozzle were exported and converted into dipole magnet strengths and particle counts for input to TOPAS. For 68 clinical cases, MC simulations were performed in a solid water phantom, and two-dimensional absolute dose distributions were measured with an ionization chamber array and compared using gamma analysis with 3%/3 mm and 2%/2 mm criteria. Results: For 47 clinical cases without daily output variation, absolute gamma passing rates compared with the pre-treatment QA plan were 99.71% and 96.97% for the two criteria; for 21 cases considering daily output variation, passing rates were 98.52% and 97.67% against plan and measurement, respectively. With a 90% passing rate threshold at the 2%/2 mm criterion, false-positive and false-negative rates fell to 11.4% and 0%. Conclusions: A log-file-based MC simulation method for patient QA of line-scanning proton therapy was successfully developed, exhibiting clinically acceptable accuracy and potential to replace measurement-based dosimetry QA at a 90% gamma passing rate threshold.
