This study aimed to evaluate the feasibility of combining helical tomotherapy (HT) and intensity-modulated proton therapy (IMPT) in treating patients with nasopharynx cancer (NPC). From January 2016 to March 2018, 98 patients received definitive radiation therapy with concurrent chemotherapy using a simultaneous integrated boost approach. All patients began with 18 HT fractions; 63 patients (64.3%) continued with HT alone for the remaining 12 fractions, while 35 patients (35.7%) switched to IMPT for the remaining 12 fractions. Grade 2 or higher mucositis occurred significantly less frequently in the HT/IMPT combination group than in the HT-only group (45.7% vs 69.8%, P = .019); grade 2 or higher analgesic usage was also less frequent, though not significantly (37.1% vs 54%, P = .110). With a median follow-up of 14 months, locoregional and distant failures occurred in 9 (9.2%) and 12 (12.2%) patients respectively, without difference by radiotherapy modality, and one-year progression-free survival was comparable between groups (81.0% vs 87.1%, P = .912). Comparable early oncologic outcomes with more favorable acute toxicity profiles were achievable by the HT/IMPT combination in treating NPC patients, while also reducing treatment costs by up to 28% compared to full-course IMPT.
