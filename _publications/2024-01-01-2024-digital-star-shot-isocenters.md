Purpose: To directly compare the radiation and imaging isocenters of a proton treatment machine, a real-time radiation isocenter verification system was developed and evaluated. Methods: The system consists of a plastic scintillator (PI-200), an acrylic phantom, a steel ball on a detachable plate, a Raspberry Pi 4 with a camera module, and analysis software implemented through a Python-based graphical user interface. After kV imaging alignment of the steel ball, the imaging isocenter, defined as the position of the steel ball, was extracted from the optical image. The proton star-shot was obtained by an optical camera because the scintillator converted the proton beam into visible light. The software then computed both the minimum circle radius and the radiation isocenter position from the star-shot, and the deviation between the imaging isocenter and radiation isocenter was calculated. Results were compared with measurements obtained by Gafchromic EBT3 film. Results: The minimum circle radii averaged 0.29 and 0.41 mm, while the position deviations from the radiation isocenter to the laser marker averaged 0.99 and 1.07 mm, for the developed system and EBT3 film, respectively. The average position difference between the radiation isocenter and imaging isocenter was 0.27 mm for the developed system, which reduced analysis time by 10 minutes. Conclusions: The system provided automated star-shot analysis with sufficient accuracy and is a cost-effective alternative to conventional film-based methods for radiation isocenter verification.
