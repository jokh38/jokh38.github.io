Background: Patient respiratory motion can significantly affect the treatment accuracy and efficiency of radiotherapy, making effective respiratory control essential. Purpose: This study examines beam-on-time (BoT) efficiency in respiratory gating proton therapy for liver cancer patients to minimize treatment duration. Methods: BoT was calculated for 101 liver cancer patients using simulated respiratory signals. BoT efficiency was defined as the ratio of BoT to total treatment time, which is the sum of BoT, layer switching times, and gating-off times for each layer. Respiratory period, layer switching time, and gating window level were varied to measure their effect on BoT efficiency. Results: Maximum BoT efficiency occurred at a specific respiratory period that correlated linearly with layer switching time. With very short switching times, efficiency remained stable across respiratory periods; when switching time exceeded 1 second, optimal efficiency aligned with the respiratory period at approximately a 1:1 slope. Conclusions: Two strategies emerged for reducing treatment time: machines with very short layer switching times show less efficiency variation, while machines with approximately 2-second switching times achieve optimal efficiency with respiration periods between 2.5 and 3 seconds, though patient-specific factors such as target size may affect the exact optimal values.
