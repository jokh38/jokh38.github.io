This study introduces dynamic range optimization, which constrains the lower limit of the minimum monitor units per spot, as a planning-based strategy to enhance efficiency in respiratory-gated proton therapy, and evaluates the resultant dosimetric trade-offs. The study analyzed 101 hepatocellular carcinoma patients who received line-scanning proton therapy. A computational framework was developed to predict total treatment time, integrating dynamic range-mediated changes in beam-on time with respiratory gating dynamics. The model was validated against fully dynamic range-optimized plans generated by RayStation v2025 across five dynamic range values (no constraint, 200, 100, 50, and 10), showing a maximum error of 10.4% compared to RayStation v2025 calculations. Decreasing dynamic range systematically reduced beam-on time in a sigmoid pattern, though treatment time reductions plateaued when layer delivery and switching completed within single gating periods. Target coverage was maintained across all dynamic range values, but normal tissue doses systematically increased with lower dynamic range values due to reduced intensity modulation capability; organs adjacent to targets experienced the largest dose increases, with gallbladder mean dose increasing 16.4% at the lowest dynamic range tested. Dynamic range optimization effectively reduces treatment time, operating independently of patient cooperation or system modifications, and a RayStation-integrated Python tool enables clinicians to identify optimal patient-specific dynamic range values, balancing efficiency gains against acceptable dosimetric quality.
