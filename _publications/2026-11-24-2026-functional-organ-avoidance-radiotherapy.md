Conventional radiotherapy planning relies on whole-organ dose-volume constraints that implicitly assume each organ at risk is functionally uniform. However, clinical dose-toxicity studies and functional imaging increasingly show that specific substructures or spatial subregions contribute disproportionately to distinct toxicities. Functional organ avoidance radiotherapy (FOART) is a treatment-planning strategy that identifies these functionally important or toxicity-relevant regions before optimization and assigns them explicit dose-reduction priority while maintaining target coverage. This narrative review summarizes contemporary FOART strategies grouped into three categories: imaging-defined functional avoidance (Type 1; functional lung avoidance radiotherapy, FLART, and differential hepatic avoidance radiotherapy, DHART), functionally relevant anatomical substructure avoidance (Type 2; hippocampal and tract-informed brain avoidance, dysphagia-aspiration risk structures (DARS) sparing, salivary gland and ductal preservation, and cardiac substructure avoidance), and spatially individualized subregional sparing (Type 3; rectal, contralateral esophagus, and pancreatic tail sparing). For each strategy, the biological rationale, imaging and delineation approach, and clinical evidence are summarized. Randomized evidence currently supports hippocampal avoidance whole-brain radiotherapy with memantine and parotid-sparing intensity-modulated radiotherapy as standard approaches. DARS sparing has phase III evidence supporting swallowing preservation. FLART, DHART, cardiac substructure avoidance, and subregional strategies have demonstrated dosimetric feasibility and selected clinical benefit but remain less mature and require further prospective, outcome-based validation before routine adoption. Further progress requires standardized functional imaging protocols, reliable deformable image registration, and rigorous prospective trial design. MRI- and PET-guided adaptive radiotherapy and AI-assisted workflows may support implementation.
