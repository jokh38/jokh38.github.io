Proton therapy can deliver a conformal dose via a steep distal dose penumbra, but the proton beam range is sensitive to uncertainty. Gamma electron vertex imaging (GEVI) has been proposed as an in-vivo range verification method using prompt gamma imaging, and a proof-of-principle system previously demonstrated the technique for therapeutic proton pencil beams without scanning, but had limitations for pencil beam scanning proton therapy. In this study, the GEVI system was upgraded and its performance improvements were tested for range-shift verification in line scanning proton treatment, demonstrating the feasibility of a high-performance GEVI system for in-vivo range verification in pencil beam scanning proton therapy.
